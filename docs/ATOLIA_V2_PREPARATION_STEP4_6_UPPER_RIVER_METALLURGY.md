# Atolia direct-NetCDF v2 preparation — Step 4.6/5

## Upper Atesis mineral landscape, mine candidates and metallurgical catchments

Status: preparation pass 4.6. This specializes Step 4.5 upstream so the metal world is generated from mineralized terrain, extraction possibilities and ore-processing chains rather than from a few named mine nodes.

Core rule:

> A mine is not a point source of copper. The latent object is a mineral occurrence embedded in terrain, exposed or concealed to some degree, discovered with some probability, exploited with some technology, beneficiated, smelted and then inserted into a river/pass/social carrier network.

The model therefore separates geological possibility, archaeological evidence and the realized v2 mining history.

## 1. Spatial domain

High-resolution metallurgical domain covers present-day South Tyrol/Alto Adige and Trentino and their immediately connected Alpine catchments, with special resolution along the upper Adige/Atesis and tributary valleys.

Do not restrict the candidate set to famous historical mines. Include every geologically plausible occurrence that could have been reachable with the period's extraction/beneficiation technology, then independently weight evidence and exploitation probability.

Primary catchment families include:

```text
upper Vinschgau / Venosta and tributary slopes
Ortler–Stilfs / Stelvio margins
Martell / Val Martello
Ulten / Val d'Ultimo
Passeier / Passiria
Meran basin tributaries
Sarntal / Sarentino
Eisack / Isarco tributaries where they feed the Atesis system
Bozen/Bolzano basin mineral corridors
Unterland / Bassa Atesina
Nonsberg / Val di Non connections
Val di Sole
Avisio system: Val di Fiemme / Fassa
Valsugana
Calisio–Trento mineral district
Primiero and eastern Trentino connections
Garda/upper Sarca connected carrier corridors
```

Catchment inclusion is hydrological/social, not modern administrative.

## 2. Geological occurrence layer

Create `/metallurgy/ore_occurrences` independently of archaeological mines.

Each occurrence stores at minimum:

```text
occurrence_id
geometry/cell
host_lithology
ore_minerals
primary_metal inventory priors
Cu/Sn/Au/Ag/Pb/Fe/As/Sb/Ni/Co/Zn priors
sulfide/oxide/carbonate fractions
vein/lens/disseminated/placer class
depth/exposure
weathering profile
fracture accessibility
water-table interaction
terrain accessibility
confidence
geological_source_id
```

Copper-bearing candidates should distinguish chalcopyrite, bornite/chalcocite where appropriate, fahlores/tetrahedrite-tennantite and secondary copper minerals because these affect trace-element and isotope/process signatures.

Tin must not be generated by simply assigning bronze demand to a nearby copper source. Tin occurrence and imported tin remain separate lineages.

Gold/silver include lode and alluvial/placer possibilities where physically admissible.

## 3. Mine-candidate ensemble

For geological occurrence `o`, time `t`, guild/community state `g`:

```text
logit P_discovery(o,t) =
    b0
  + b_exposure * surface_exposure
  + b_colour   * visible_mineralization
  + b_stream   * downstream_indicator_access
  + b_route    * human_landscape_contact
  + b_prior    * nearby_successful_extraction
  + b_skill    * prospecting_skill(g,t)
  - b_cover    * overburden
  - b_access   * terrain_cost
```

Discovery does not imply profitable mining.

```text
P_exploit = f(
  grade,
  accessible_ore_mass,
  excavation_cost,
  fuel/charcoal access,
  water,
  labour,
  local metallurgical skill,
  transport cost,
  metal demand/value,
  competing deposits,
  political/conflict pressure
)
```

Thus hundreds/thousands of occurrences can exist while only a subset become active mines in a given realization.

## 4. Extraction technology stack

Mining capabilities evolve autonomously with guild ecology. Candidate operations can use combinations of:

```text
surface collection
stream/placer recovery
open cut
shallow pit
trench following vein
stone/antler/wood tool excavation
hammerstone breaking
fire-setting where admissible
wooden support
simple drainage/bailing
shaft/adit only when technology/terrain permits
ore sorting at face
hand cobbing
crushing/grinding
washing/gravity separation
roasting
```

Store the actual operation sequence. Do not replace it with a scalar `mine_efficiency`.

## 5. Mine lifetime

Mines emerge, intensify, decline, reopen and disappear.

```text
prospect -> trial extraction -> active -> expanded -> depleted/blocked -> dormant -> rediscovered/reopened
```

Hazards include:

```text
ore exhaustion at accessible depth
water ingress
collapse
fuel depletion
labour shortage
conflict
transport disruption
better competing discovery
loss of specialist skill
poor smelting yield
```

Reopening may occur generations later with different technology and guild lineage.

## 6. Ore is not metal

Track explicitly:

```text
ore extracted
waste rock
beneficiated concentrate
roasted concentrate
smelting charge
slag
matte/intermediate where process requires
raw copper/metal
refined/remelted metal
finished object metal
```

Mass conservation is checked at every transformation.

This is essential because the transport graph differs radically depending on whether tonnes of low-grade ore or much smaller quantities of concentrate/metal are moved.

## 7. Processing location choice

For each extraction episode choose where successive operations occur based on mass reduction, fuel, water and transport.

```text
mine-face sorting: usually near mine
crushing/washing: mine or water-access site
roasting: ore/fuel compromise
smelting: ore + charcoal + clay/refractory + water + labour compromise
refining/remelting: increasingly settlement/workshop/market linked
```

This generates processing corridors rather than assuming mine = smelter = workshop.

## 8. Upper-river carrier coupling

Every ore/metal packet is coupled directly to Step 4/4.5 hydrology.

Mountain movement first follows physically admissible valley/path networks. Once material reaches a navigable river/lake/wetland carrier, water-mode probability can rise sharply because bulk ore/concentrate benefits more from transport efficiency than finished prestige goods.

But navigability is realization-specific. The model must never assume the modern Adige was uniformly navigable upstream.

Maintain distinct cumulative distances:

```text
ore_distance
concentrate_distance
raw_metal_distance
object_distance
recycled_metal_distance
lineage_total_distance
```

The final artefact can therefore have a short object biography but a very long metal biography.

## 9. Downstream indicator field

Prospecting should exploit hydrology without magical geological knowledge.

Mineral occurrences can produce weak downstream indicators through erosion and stream sediments.

```text
indicator(x,t,element/mineral) = transport_and_decay(upstream occurrences)
```

Prospecting guilds/communities encountering anomalous stream sediments receive increased discovery probability upstream.

This provides a physically meaningful way for mining knowledge to spread through river landscapes.

## 10. Named districts are evidence anchors, not exclusive sources

Known mining/metallurgical districts should be entered as higher-evidence anchors where supported, including the major Trentino copper-mining landscapes such as Monte Calisio and other securely documented prehistoric extraction/smelting zones, while South Tyrolean historical mineral districts and mineral occurrences constrain geology without automatically being labelled Bronze Age mines.

Each named place receives two independent records where necessary:

```text
GEOLOGICAL_OCCURRENCE evidence
ARCHAEOLOGICAL_EXPLOITATION evidence
```

A strong geological occurrence with no prehistoric mining evidence remains a candidate, not an observed prehistoric mine.

Conversely slag/smelting evidence can constrain a processing zone even if the exact extraction face is unknown.

## 11. South Tyrol specificity

The upper Atesis world should actively test mineralized tributary catchments rather than treating the Venosta–Bolzano axis as a blank transport corridor.

High-resolution candidate generation should include mineral occurrence databases and geological mapping across Vinschgau/Venosta, Passeier, Sarntal, Eisack-connected mineral belts, Bolzano basin margins, Ulten and Ortler/Stilfs-connected terrain.

Schneeberg/Monteneve and other famous later mining districts may constrain mineral geology and route ecology, but later documentary mining intensity must not be projected backwards as Bronze Age production.

This distinction is mandatory in provenance fields.

## 12. Trentino specificity

Trentino receives similarly dense treatment, especially the Calisio/Trento, Valsugana, Avisio/Fiemme and eastern/southern connected metallurgical landscapes.

Where archaeological evidence supports prehistoric copper extraction, beneficiation, smelting or slag, encode the observation with chronology uncertainty. Where only mineral geology supports exploitation, keep it inferred.

Do not make one famous district supply the entire regional copper budget merely because its evidence survives better.

## 13. 200 kt Atesis-sourced copper constraint

The Step-1 v2 target of roughly 200 kt copper associated with the Atesis world over the full millennium is a regional mass-budget experiment, not permission to invent ore bodies.

The builder must first calculate the physically available accessible copper inventory from the occurrence ensemble.

Then report:

```text
requested_Atesis_Cu_mass
geologically_available_accessible_Cu
realized_extracted_Cu
realized_metallic_Cu_after_losses
fraction from observed prehistoric mines
fraction from geologically supported inferred mines
fraction requiring imported copper
```

If the geology/technology cannot support 200 kt, the model must fail or import the deficit rather than silently scale deposits upward.

## 14. Other metals

The broader v2 world target (~1 Mt Cu distributed through other river systems, ~30 kt Sn plus Au/Ag and relevant alloy/trace metals) uses the same occurrence/extraction formalism.

No metal is generated as a recipe ingredient without lineage.

For each atomically represented packet/lineage retain source mixture pointers and processing history sufficient for Step-3 isotope/trace calculations.

## 15. Metallurgical guild coupling

Step-2 guilds interact with the mine world through actual skills:

```text

```

Different communities need not possess the whole chain. Specialist networks can therefore emerge naturally.

Skill quality changes recovery, impurity partitioning, slag chemistry, product quality and mine profitability.

## 16. Skill evolution from feedback

A workshop/mining lineage learns from repeated outcomes:

```text
ore appearance -> process choice -> yield -> slag/metal observation -> retained practice
```

High-quality continuous production stabilizes a guild lineage. Persistent low yield, brittle metal, fuel inefficiency or mine failure lowers survival/recruitment.

Knowledge can migrate with craftspeople and apprentices independently of ore/metal movement.

## 17. Full metallurgical history pointer chain

Every final object must be able to trace:

```text
object
 -> casting/forging episode
 -> alloy batch
 -> remelt batch(es)
 -> prior object/scrap lineages
 -> refining batch
 -> smelting batch
 -> concentrate batch
 -> beneficiation episode
 -> extraction episode
 -> occurrence(s)
 -> geological/isotope inventory
```

This is pointer-based in NetCDF. Never duplicate the entire history into every object row.

## 18. Recycling and source mixtures

Recycling may combine metals from different mines and river systems.

A source mixture is sparse:

```text
source_ptr[n_lineage+1]
source_index[n_components]
source_mass_fraction[n_components]
```

The harmonic/geometric/arithmetic/quadratic diagnostics operate on the true mixture without clipping the harmonic lens to 1.

Repeated recycling naturally increases source entropy and lineage distance, while pristine grave goods may retain a narrow source signature.

## 19. Slag as persistent archaeological truth

Smelting creates slag/debris that does not travel like metal.

Store slag deposits as their own persistent spatial populations:

```text
slag_id
process_episode
location
mass
chemistry/mineralogy
chronology
postdepositional state
```

This gives the later game a route for inferring vanished metallurgical activity independently from finished-object distributions.

## 20. Charcoal/fuel ecology

Metallurgy consumes landscape resources.

Each smelting/roasting episode draws fuel from a catchment with regeneration and transport cost.

```text
wood/charcoal demand
fuel catchment radius
species/wood-density proxy
regrowth
competing household demand
```

Sustained high production can shift processing sites, enlarge fuel catchments or make another deposit economically preferable.

This prevents impossible perpetual megamines with zero environmental footprint.

## 21. Water and season

Mining and beneficiation depend on seasonal hydrology.

```text

stream flow for washing
river transport season
charcoal-production season
```

The annual simulation may use seasonal substeps for mining/process decisions while retaining coarser long-term world snapshots.

## 22. Conflict and ownership

Ore districts are resources in the Step-4 war/social field, but ownership is not imposed as modern territorial polygons.

Conflict can change mine operators without changing geological truth.

## 24. Data-efficiency rule

Fifty million represented objects do not receive fifty million duplicated mine histories.

Shared process batches and lineages are interned:

```text
many objects -> alloy batch
many alloy batches -> metal lineage/profile
many lineages -> smelting/refining batches
many batches -> sparse source mixtures
```

Only divergent events create new records.

This is the same ECMWF-style principle that reduced the v1 runtime from exact-state scale to reusable profiles.

## 26. Falsification diagnostics

Specifically test whether the desired world requires pathological assumptions.

Flag if:

```text
one deposit supplies implausibly dominant regional mass
mine extraction exceeds accessible geological inventory
smelting yield exceeds process capability
fuel demand exceeds plausible catchment supply
ore is transported absurd distances before beneficiation
all inferred mines conveniently lie on desired transport corridors
200 kt Atesis copper is met only by hidden unsupported deposits
source mixtures homogenize unrealistically fast
all guilds acquire identical mining/metallurgical skills
```

These are failures, not tuning opportunities to conceal.

## 27. Career relevance

The player never sees this entire truth table at level 1.

A level-1 artefact can expose only instrument-scale consequences:

```text
alloy chemistry
microstructure
corrosion
trace elements
isotope measurement
manufacturing marks
```

The hidden lineage already knows the ore occurrences, processing batches, recycling and transport history. Guild progression and real-paper missions gradually provide methods capable of resolving those layers.

Thus the late-game reconstruction is genuinely recovering a world that existed before the player's career crystallized.

## 28. Step-4.6 invariants

1. Geological occurrence != prehistoric mine.
2. Famous later mines do not become Bronze Age mines by projection.
3. Known prehistoric extraction/smelting evidence remains privileged evidence, not the only allowed possibility.
4. Mine discovery and exploitation are separate stochastic processes.
5. Ore, concentrate, slag and metal conserve mass separately.
6. Processing locations emerge from ore/fuel/water/transport/skill tradeoffs.
7. Upper-river hydrology controls bulk-material transport opportunities.
8. Tin and alloy additions always retain independent source lineage.
9. Recycling can mix multiple geological sources without destroying pointer history.
10. Guild skills modify real process outcomes and survival.
11. Slag/debris remain spatial archaeological populations.
12. The 200 kt Atesis copper target cannot override geology.
13. Unsupported mass deficits must become imports or explicit model failure.
14. Metallurgical histories are shared/pointer-based, not duplicated per object.
15. Atoliamaxx compatibility has no write access to source generation.

## 29. Step 5 handoff

The final v2 builder now receives an upstream physical chain:

```text
mineralized geology
    ↓
prospecting/discovery
    ↓
active mine ecology
    ↓
beneficiation + fuel + water
    ↓
smelting/refining
    ↓
metal batches + isotope/trace truth
    ↓
guild manufacturing
    ↓
carrier/river/boat movement
    ↓
repair + broker + recycling
    ↓
loss/deposition/findspot
```

The intended consequence is that a sword found hundreds of kilometres downstream is no longer assigned a convenient `source`. Its metal can have been extracted from several Alpine occurrences, beneficiated beside a tributary, smelted elsewhere, moved down the Atesis, cast, carried, repaired, looted, remelted with imported scrap and finally deposited — while the NetCDF retains enough sparse pointers to reconstruct every one of those transformations.
